> This guide explains how to customize Windows using its built-in accessibility tools. Originally designed for specific disabilities, these settings improve the computing experience for everyone.

## Introduction

The **Accessibility menu** (formerly "Ease of Access") is the hub for these tools. It groups features by vision, hearing, dexterity, and cognitive needs.

---

## How to Access the Menu

Open Accessibility settings using one of these methods:

*   **Keyboard Shortcut:** Press **Win + U**.
*   **Settings App:** Go to **Start > Settings > Accessibility** (Windows 11) or **Ease of Access** (Windows 10).

## Vision Tools

*Essential for low vision, color blindness, or eye fatigue.*

*   **Text & Scaling:** Increase text size in **Accessibility > Text size**, or scale the entire interface in **System > Display > Scale**.
*   **Text Cursor Indicator:** Add a bright color to your cursor in **Accessibility > Text cursor**.
*   **Magnifier:** Zoom in by pressing **Win + Plus (+)**.
*   **Narrator:** Toggle the built-in screen reader with **Win + Ctrl + Enter**.
*   **Color Filters & High Contrast:** Apply color-blindness filters or high-contrast themes in **Accessibility > Color filters** or **Contrast themes**.
*   **Pointer Customization:** Enlarge or recolor the mouse pointer in **Accessibility > Mouse pointer and touch**.

## Hearing Tools

*Helpful for hearing loss or noisy environments.*

*   **Live Captions:** Transcribe spoken audio automatically. Enable this in **Accessibility > Captions** or press **Win + Ctrl + L**.
*   **Mono Audio:** Combine left and right stereo channels in **Accessibility > Audio**.
*   **Visual Alerts:** Flash your screen or active window for audio notifications in **Accessibility > Audio**.

![A demonstration of live captions transcribing spoken audio in real-time](/img/accessibility/captions-example.jpg)

## Dexterity & Mobility Tools

*Useful for limited mobility, tremors, or hands-free control.*

*   **Voice Access/Typing:** Control your PC and dictate text. Press **Win + H** to type with your voice, or enable voice control in **Accessibility > Speech**.
*   **Sticky Keys:** Press key combinations (like Ctrl + Alt + Del) one key at a time. Press **Shift** five times to toggle, or go to **Accessibility > Keyboard**.
*   **On-Screen Keyboard:** Open a virtual keyboard with **Win + Ctrl + O** or in **Accessibility > Keyboard**.
*   **Eye Control:** Navigate using eye-tracking hardware in **Accessibility > Eye control**.

![A user interacting with a device using voice commands, demonstrating hands-free control](/img/accessibility/voice-assistant-example.jpg)

## Cognitive & Focus Tools

*Ideal for minimizing distractions and improving concentration.*

*   **Focus Sessions:** Block distractions by enabling "Do Not Disturb" and silencing taskbar badges in **System > Focus**.
*   **Disable Animations:** Reduce motion and improve performance by turning off transparency and animations in **Accessibility > Visual effects**.
*   **Night Light:** Limit blue light to reduce eye strain. Go to **System > Display > Night light**.
*   **Taskbar Simplification:** Hide unnecessary icons in **Settings > Personalization > Taskbar**.
*   **Immersive Reader:** Remove visual clutter from web pages in Microsoft Edge by clicking the **Immersive Reader icon** in the address bar.

## Detailed Setup Guide

Here is how to set up Narrator and Voice Access, the core tools for screen-reading and hands-free control.

### 1. Setting Up Narrator (Screen Reader)
Narrator reads your screen aloud so you can navigate without a mouse.

*   **Quick Start/Stop:** Press **Win + Ctrl + Enter**.
*   **Access Settings:** Press **Win + Ctrl + N** to open Narrator settings.
*   **Key Customizations:**
    *   **Natural Voices:** In **Settings > Accessibility > Narrator**, select **Add** under "Add natural voices" for realistic speech.
    *   **Auto-Start:** Check **Start Narrator after sign-in** or **Start Narrator before sign-in** to launch it automatically.
    *   **Verbosity:** Control how much detail Narrator provides (like button names) under the **Verbosity** section.

### 2. Setting Up Voice Access (Windows 11)
Voice Access lets you control your PC and dictate text completely hands-free. It processes audio locally and works offline. *(Note: Windows 10 users have a similar feature called Windows Speech Recognition.)*

*   **Turn it On:** Go to **Settings > Accessibility > Speech** and toggle **Voice access** to **On**.
*   **Initial Setup:**
    1. Select your microphone.
    2. Wait for the system to download the speech model.
    3. Complete the interactive guide to learn commands like "Open Notepad" or "Click Start".
*   **Basic Commands:**
    *   **Wake Up/Sleep:** Say "Voice access wake up" to listen, or "Voice access sleep" to pause.
    *   **Interacting:** Say "Show numbers" to label clickable items. Then say "Click [number]" to select one.
    *   **Grid Control:** Say "Show grid" to overlay a grid for precise mouse targeting.

### Comparison
*   **Narrator:** Choose this if you need the computer to read content to you.
*   **Voice Access:** Choose this if you need to operate the computer hands-free.
